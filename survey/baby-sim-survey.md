## Section 1 — Introduction (read, ~30 sec)

**Baby Sim: An Interactive Simulator for Practicing Parenting Responses**

Thank you for taking part. Baby Sim is an early-stage venture founded by a Harvard student. It is a browser-based simulation that lets expectant parents, parents, and caregivers rehearse everyday parenting situations with a virtual child, and see how different responses may affect the child across developmental stages.

**How the simulation works**
1. **Set up:** Create a virtual child and select a developmental stage: infancy, early childhood, preschool, school age, or adolescence.
2. **Scenario:** Encounter an age-appropriate everyday situation, such as an infant's cry, a kitchen accident, anxiety before a school presentation, or a conversation about phone use.
3. **Response:** Choose from five response options that span the range of parenting approaches, from supportive and structured to harsh or harmful.
4. **Analysis:** Review how the child may experience the moment, which developmental areas are involved (for example, trust, autonomy, or confidence), and suggestions for repairing or strengthening the interaction.

**Evidence base:** The response analysis draws on education and parenting-professional frameworks, established developmental theories, and published research evidence. It is intended for education and reflection and is not a clinical assessment, diagnosis, or judgment of any individual's parenting.

**About this survey:** We are gathering early feedback on the concept and prototype. The survey takes approximately 5–8 minutes. Participation is voluntary and anonymous, you may skip any optional question, and you may stop at any time. Providing contact information at the end is optional.
